Undergraduate Capstone Thesis

FOVB-AIoT Research & Innovation

Four-in-One Vital Sign Sensors with BMI Calculation using AI and IoT for Health Risk Prediction in RTU Pasig Clinic.

Official Abstract
Rizal Technological University • March 2026

This study presents the development of the FOVB-AIoT, a kiosk-based health monitoring system that integrates a Four-in-One Vital Sign Sensor with Body Mass Index (BMI) calculation using Artificial Intelligence (AI) and the Internet of Things (IoT) for Health Risk Prediction. Designed to improve efficiency and accuracy over traditional manual screening methods in educational and institutional health clinics, the FOVB-AIoT platform measures key physiological parameters—including body temperature, heart rate, respiratory rate, oxygen saturation (SpO2), and blood pressure (via AI computer vision)—while simultaneously computing BMI using automated laser LiDAR height and digital load cell weight sensors.

An RFID-based fast-track system enables rapid authentication without repetitive manual data entry. Telemetry data are securely transmitted to an IoT web portal for real-time access by clinic personnel. The integrated AI engine analyzes vital sign patterns to detect anomalous physiological signs and calculate a Comprehensive Risk Score, providing actionable health recommendations and automated thermal slips to support proactive preventive care.

Keywords: FOVB-AIoT RTU Pasig Clinic AI Computer Vision (YOLO) Comprehensive Risk Score IoT Health Database RFID Authentication

The Innovation Story

How We Developed & Innovated FOVB-AIoT

A journey of problem discovery, late-night laboratory prototyping, custom machine learning training, and university deployment.

Phase 1: Inception & Discovery August 2025

Identifying Clinic Queues & Manual Bottlenecks

Field observations and interviews at the Rizal Technological University - Pasig Campus Health Clinic revealed significant operational friction: nurses spent excessive time manually measuring and recording body temperature, blood pressure, pulse, and BMI calculation during peak semester hours. We conceptualized an all-in-one contactless station capable of capturing vital signs in under 60 seconds.

Stakeholder Consultations Workflow Bottleneck Analysis Initial Architecture Draft
Phase 2: Sensor Selection & Circuit Design October 2025

Engineering Multi-Modal Sensor Fusion

We selected and benchmarked medical-grade and industrial sensors: MLX90614 infrared thermopile for non-contact thermal readings, MAX30102 photoplethysmography for heart rate and blood oxygenation, TF-Luna time-of-flight LiDAR for automated height measurement, and HX711 with industrial load cells for body mass. Dual microcontrollers (Arduino Mega 2560 and Arduino Nano) were configured to prevent serial buffer bottlenecking.

Dual Arduino Interfacing I2C / UART Bus Design Sensor Calibration Rigs
Phase 3: AI Computer Vision & OCR December 2025

Overcoming the Blood Pressure Digitization Challenge

Rather than attempting to tamper with sealed medical sphygmomanometer electronics (which violates medical safety compliance), our team engineered an optical computer vision enclosure. We mounted an HD camera and trained a custom YOLOv8 object detection model on thousands of 7-segment digital LCD photos, achieving reliable extraction of systolic, diastolic, and pulse readings across harsh lighting variations.

YOLOv8 7-Segment Model Optical Glare Shielding 98.4% OCR Precision
Phase 4: Full-Stack IoT Platform & Hardware Integration January 2026

Building the Kiosk Enclosure, Thermal Printer & Web Portal

We fabricated the physical standalone ergonomic kiosk with an embedded Dell Mini PC, 15.6" capacitive touchscreen, and a 58mm thermal receipt printer. The web portal was developed featuring real-time telemetry syncing, multi-parameter AI risk prediction, digital RFID pass generation, and individual student/staff dashboards.

Physical Enclosure Fabrication Thermal Printer ESC/POS Integration Dynamic RFID Smart Pass
Phase 5: Clinical Validation & Capstone Defense February – March 2026

Clinical Verification & Successful University Presentation

The FOVB-AIoT kiosk was tested with real Rizalian students, faculty, and clinic staff. Sensor telemetry was systematically compared against clinical mercury sphygmomanometers and clinical thermometers, proving high diagnostic reliability, high user satisfaction, and dramatic reduction in screening time from minutes down to under 60 seconds.

Clinical Gold-Standard Comparison Real Campus Pilot Testing Successful Capstone Defense
System Architecture

Physical Kiosk Hardware & Web Portal Architecture

Distinguishing the physical sensor screening station at the RTU Pasig Clinic from this online documentation and student records portal.

1. Physical Kiosk Station

  • Dell Mini PC (Intel Core i5 10th Gen): Standalone processing unit inside the physical kiosk managing real-time sensor streams and local displays.
  • Arduino Mega 2560: Primary microcontroller managing data streams from the LiDAR, load cells, temperature, and pulse oximeter.
  • Arduino Nano: Secondary microcontroller dedicated to the custom blood pressure enclosure, controlling LCD feedback and LED indicators.
  • Fortress CF160TM 15.6" Touchscreen: High-definition capacitive interactive user interface for guided screening sessions.
  • Embedded Thermal Receipt Printer: Instantly issues printed clinical vital slips for patient records and physical clinic referrals.

2. Physical Kiosk Sensors

  • MLX90614 Infrared Sensor: Non-contact radiometric temperature sensor ensuring medical hygiene and fast reading.
  • MAX30102 PPG Sensor: Photoplethysmography sensor extracting Heart Rate, SpO2 blood oxygen, and respiratory rhythm.
  • TF-Luna LiDAR Sensor: Laser time-of-flight sensor overhead for accurate, contactless height detection.
  • HX711 & Load Cell Platform: Industrial analog-to-digital weight sensor converting mechanical force to accurate mass for BMI.
  • RC522 RFID 13.56 MHz Reader: Contactless smart card interface on the kiosk console for 1-tap student/staff profile recognition.

3. Online Web Portal & Archive

  • Thesis Archive & Memories: Open documentation, complete 194 MB research manuscript download, and development timeline.
  • USB RFID Registration: Connect any plug-and-play USB RFID reader to your laptop to scan and link your student card.
  • Student Health History Dashboard: Access your historical screening records, longitudinal trend charts, and official RTU clinic slips.
  • AI Risk Assessment Engine: Evaluates multi-parameter physiological patterns into a Comprehensive Health Risk Score.
